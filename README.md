# Aesthetic VAWT Design

A kinetic-sculpture wind turbine designed for ENGE 1216 (Engineering Design, Virginia Tech, Spring 2026) — built to double as a piece of backyard art and a supplemental home electricity source.

## The Brief

Our team was tasked, via a project brief from fictional client **Art Power, Inc.**, with designing a vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) framed as a *kinetic sculpture* — something a middle-class homeowner would want sitting in their backyard, not just a piece of utility hardware. The brief's core tension: most wind turbines are functional but not attractive, and most kinetic sculptures are beautiful but not affordable or effective at generating power. Our job was to solve for both at once.

**Constraints from the client:**
- Spins around a central vertical axis
- Resists local weather conditions year-round
- Enjoyable to view for all ages, including children and seniors
- Requires minimal maintenance once installed
- Safe to operate around children, pets, and the environment
- Must not exceed **2 ft** in sculpted height
- Target retail cost under **$150** (labor, overhead, and profit included)
- Must demonstrate measurable electricity generation
- Sold in kit form for at-home assembly

The single most important goal driving our design decisions: the turbine had to look good *and* generate power efficiently — aesthetics wasn't a secondary nice-to-have, it was the differentiator the whole brief was built around.

## Our Approach

- Researched VAWT types and generated 6 candidate concepts as a 6-person team (see `docs/`)
- Down-selected to a final direction using early sketching and prototyping (`concepts/Early_Bird_Designs/`, `docs/Design_Map`, `docs/Selecting_Mid-Level_Prototypes`)
- Ran an aesthetics survey to validate the visual direction against real viewer preferences before committing to a final build (`surveys/`)
- Landed on a **9-blade modular, pine-cone-inspired rotor** — a design that reads as sculptural from any angle while balancing energy output against the 2 ft height limit and kit-assembly requirement
- Estimated manufacturing/kit cost against the client's $150 target (`cost/wind_turbine_cost_estimate.xlsx`)
- Built and validated a working prototype on a test rig, then presented final results (`presentation/Final_Presentation.pptx`)

## Results

- Measured power output across a **2.1–4.4 m/s** wind-speed range on a physical test rig
- Final grade: **A+**
- Test footage in `media/`
